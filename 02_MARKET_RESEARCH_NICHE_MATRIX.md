## Deeper look at the finalist: ADHD/neurodivergent digital planning systems

Evidence pulled directly (not estimated):
- A single ADHD Notion template listing on Etsy has **7,727 favorites** and
  documented sustained sales volume over 6 months (Money Cornucopia case study).
- Premium tier for ADHD-specific "systems" (bundles, Life OS dashboards, gamified
  planners) sells at **$30–$80**, well above the generic Notion template range of
  $10–$15 — buyers pay a specificity premium.
- Buyer language consistently centers on *"a system that understands how my brain
  works"* — not features, but felt understanding. This is a defensible emotional
  moat generic productivity sellers can't copy without doing the same research.
- Category is digital-only: **87-95% gross margin before ad spend** per POD-vs-
  digital research (vs. 20-40% for POD physical goods).
- Low seasonality (evergreen; mild back-to-school/January bump, not a cliff).
- Direct expansion path: daily/weekly variants, meal planning, medication/habit
  tracking, finance, "low-spoons mode," student vs. professional vs. parent
  editions, print-friendly vs. Notion vs. GoodNotes formats, plus the adjacent
  autism/homeschool audiences already scored above.

**Why not pet portraits (#3 raw score, high demand)?** Competitive intensity is
low-scored (heavy incumbent presence, commoditizing on style rather than
substance) and repeat-purchase potential is weak — a customer buys one portrait
of their pet, rarely a second. ADHD planning systems have both a stronger
differentiation moat (buyer-language evidence above) and a natural repeat-
purchase/expansion structure (an operating "life system" people add modules to
over months), which the objective explicitly weights ("one winning insight
should create many SKUs").
